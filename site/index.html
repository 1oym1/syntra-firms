In 2012, Sudan's fires didn't double. The satellites changed.

NASA has watched fires from space since 2000, first with MODIS, then with the sharper VIIRS. Stitch the two together and you get a fake jump. SYNTRA turns both into one record you can trust, from 2000 to this week.

The problem

Two good satellites, one broken record

1 km

MODIS, since 2000

Terra and Aqua give the long history, but their coarse pixels miss small fires.

375 m

VIIRS, since 2012

S-NPP, NOAA-20 and NOAA-21 see about seven times more pixels per area, so they count far more fires.

×2.5

A jump that isn't real

Joining the two raw counts makes 2012 look 2.5 times worse than 2011. Any trend or anomaly built on that is wrong.

How it works

Four steps from raw hotspots to one record

STEP 1

Download

Yearly FIRMS archives for MODIS and VIIRS, plus the near-real-time API for this year.

4.46 M hotspots, 2000–2026
STEP 2

Clean

Same confidence rule for both sensors, clip to the border, and set aside gas flares, industry and other always-hot sites.

45,464 static hotspots removed
STEP 3

Harmonize

Count fire cell-days on a 0.1° grid, then convert MODIS to VIIRS units with a factor for each calendar month, fitted on the 2012–2021 overlap.

NOAA-20 × 0.986 after S-NPP ended
STEP 4

Validate

Leave one year out: fit on the other years, predict the hidden one, compare with what VIIRS actually saw.

R² 0.993 vs 0.36 raw

Does it hold up?

Tested on held-out years, and beyond Sudan

Accuracy on years the model never saw, 119 months each. It works where vegetation burns. In Saudi Arabia, with few real fires and many gas flares, it does not, and we say so.
