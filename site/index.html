    Continuity monitor

    A record that outlives MODIS

    Satellites retire. Every time one hands over to another, a fire record can break, as it did in 2012. SYNTRA keeps the whole record in VIIRS units and fits each handover on the years both satellites flew, tests it on years it never saw, and keeps checking it against the live feed.

    MODIS is retiring

    When MODIS goes dark, this record does not break

    NASA plans to stop collecting Terra MODIS science data around January 2027, and Aqua's end date is still under review (NASA LAADS DAAC, MODIS to VIIRS transition). Any fire record that mixes raw MODIS with VIIRS will jump again. SYNTRA's does not: 2000 to 2011 is already in VIIRS units, so the series carries on unchanged on NOAA-20 and NOAA-21.

    Under the hood

    From raw pixels to a record you can plan with

    DOWNLOAD4,462,802

    clean hotspots, 2000–2026, from the FIRMS archive and the near-real-time API.

    CLEAN45,464

    detections at always-hot sites set aside: gas flares, industry and other static heat sources.

    HARMONIZE0.1°

    fire cell-days: each grid cell counts once a day, then MODIS is converted month by month.

    VALIDATER² 0.993

    leave-one-year-out on 119 months. NOAA-20 × 0.986 takes over where Suomi NPP data ends.

    Who SYNTRA serves

    One fire record, many decisions

    The same consistent record answers different questions for different people. Each item links to where it works on this page today.

    NASA

    Earth science and applied sciences

    Terra MODIS is due to stop collecting science data around January 2027. SYNTRA shows a fire record can stay unbroken through that switch, built on NASA FIRMS and NASA POWER, for agriculture, disasters and health.

    • Continuity monitor and live drift check
    • NOAA-21 on standby as the next handover

    Agriculture and food security

    Ministry of Agriculture · FAO · WFP

    Most of Sudan's fires are crop leftovers burned after harvest, so the record is also a farming calendar seen from space. A late start points to a late harvest; a farm area that stops burning is a sign of land-use change worth checking.

    • Fire type: farm burning or something more intense
    • Risk clock: when the burning season peaks

    Public health

    Ministry of Health · WHO

    The October to December peak puts smoke over towns and cities. Knowing the weeks and the areas lets health teams prepare clinics for more breathing-related cases.

    • The high-fire weeks of the year
    • Humidity and wind against normal

    Civil defence and disaster response

    Civil defence · disaster management

    One call a day, made from the satellites, the 26-year baseline and the weather: expected seasonal burning, or a short list of areas to check first.

    • Early warning with the areas to check
    • Live NASA FIRMS feed, every 30 minutes

    Environment and protected areas

    Environment and forestry bodies

    A fire where land rarely burns means something. The baseline tells routine burning from the unusual, area by area, which matters most in reserves such as Dinder National Park.

    • Fires where they are rare

    Humanitarian agencies

    UN and relief organisations

    Fires that break the usual pattern show where to look first. SYNTRA flags them as a reason to check, never as a claim about what caused them.

    • Areas to check, with a satellite view link

    Research and climate

    Universities · climate studies

    One consistent series from 2000 means a trend is real, not the 2012 satellite switch. Questions like "is the fire season starting earlier?" become answerable.

    • Any month since 2000, in one set of units

    Every country with a fire season

    Beyond Sudan

    The method is not tied to Sudan: on held-out months it scores R² 0.986 in Ethiopia and 0.905 in Iraq. Where real fires are too few, as in Saudi Arabia, it says so instead of guessing.

    • Tested on four countries
